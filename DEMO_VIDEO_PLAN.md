## Narration draft

Closing the books should not mean guessing which payment belongs to which invoice. Ma'at brings the invoice, payment, and original evidence into one private workspace.

After signing in, a new account starts empty. Let's create a reconciliation and upload two fictional files prepared for this recording: invoices and payments. The app previews the columns, lets us check their mapping, and validates the rows before processing them.

The matching worker compares references, currencies, and amounts. Clear exact matches are allocated automatically. Partial payments and uncertain candidates stay visible for review.

Here is an invoice for fifteen hundred dollars and a payment for one thousand dollars. We can inspect the original CSV rows alongside the normalized values. Nothing changes the imported source evidence.

Vultr Serverless Inference explains the exception using the supplied rows. The response is labelled as AI advice, with evidence references and limitations. Financial totals come from deterministic rules and confirmed allocations; the model cannot change them.

After checking both source rows, I confirm the partial allocation. This is an accounting classification, not a transfer. Five hundred dollars remains outstanding on this invoice.

The report now reflects the confirmed decision, and it still includes unresolved invoices and unallocated payments. We can download the CSV records for follow-up.

If a decision needs correcting, Undo adds a reversal to the history. The original action remains traceable, and the balances update.

The workspace adapts to both desktop and phone screens. Each account sees its own uploaded records. Ma'at turns two CSVs into a clear, reviewable close, with the evidence and every decision still attached.
